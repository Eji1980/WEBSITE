You are holding a lot

“How do you lead when you are struggling yourself?”

  • Everyone turns to you, and you are always last on your own list.
  • It feels easier to do it yourself than to hand it over.
  • You say yes when you mean no, then wonder where the time or energy will come from.
  • You feel you have to be bulletproof, so you keep a steady face while quietly running on empty.
  • You rehearse the conversation you need to have, but keep putting it off.
  • You wonder: is it me, or is it the job?

None of this makes you a weak leader. Usually it means you care, and nobody showed you how to lead others without losing yourself. You can be committed to your team and still need support, clearer limits and room to breathe.

The Inside-Out approach

Is it me, or is it the job? Usually both. So we work with both.

Most coaching looks only inward. Most leadership advice looks only at the role. I bring the two together: lasting change begins within, and becomes real in the way you lead.

Within you

The stories you carry

The stories we tell ourselves about responsibility, worth and what a good leader should be shape how we respond. “If I don't, who will?” “A good leader always has the answer.” When we see those stories clearly, we find choices we could not see before.

Around you

The role you lead in

We also look honestly at the demands of the role, how work flows through your team, the agreements with your boss and the resources you have. I've worked inside these systems for 20 years. Some things call for a new perspective. Others call for a new agreement, more support or a change in conditions.

  1. Notice what is happening

    Make space to hear yourself beneath the urgency and expectations: what you feel, what you believe, what matters to you.

  2. Explore a new perspective

    Through reflection, coaching, storytelling and practical leadership wisdom, discover another way to see the situation.

  3. Try it in your life

    Choose one meaningful next step: a conversation, a handover, a boundary or a request for support. Notice what happens.

Your life becomes the laboratory. If you've worked in Agile teams, you'll recognise the rhythm: a small experiment, an honest look at what happened, then the next step. I bring my experience, but I don't teach you my way. I help you discover yours.

A few questions you may have

Do I need to know exactly what I want to work on?

No. You can begin with what feels heavy, confusing or important. We can use the Discovery Conversation to explore what support might help.

I have no time. Is coaching realistic?

Sessions are online and focused. You can choose a single 90-minute session, or six sessions at your own pace.

What if the problem is my organisation, not me?

That may well be part of the picture. We look at what you can influence, what needs to be shared or raised elsewhere, and which choices are available. You do not have to make every problem your responsibility.

How is coaching different from a masterclass?

Coaching centres on your own situation and unfolds through conversation and practice. A masterclass explores one focused theme in a shared, live experience. Begin with whichever feels right.

Is this therapy?

No. Coaching focuses on discovery, choices and putting change into practice. It can sit alongside therapeutic or medical support, not instead of it.

What might become possible with more room for you?

You don't need to have the answer yet. We can begin with a conversation.
